01 · The principle

Renovation is really subtraction, addition and restraint.

Improving an existing property means understanding what's there before changing anything. The structure, services and finishes have already been decided once. Renovation is about deciding, carefully, what stays and what changes.

That's why we start with the property — not with a wish list. The right scope for a renovation is the one that respects the building's reality and serves the client's actual needs.

The workflow

How a renovation moves forward.

A renovation is a sequence — understand first, plan carefully, then change. These five stages describe how the work is structured.

Stage 01

Assess

Understand the existing property — structure, services, finishes, constraints and what the client actually needs.

Stage 02

Plan

Define the intended changes, layout, materials and scope — agreed before any work begins.

Stage 03

Coordinate

Organise materials, trades and site sequence so the work moves without disruption.

Stage 04

Build

Execute the agreed changes — carefully, within the existing shell and to the agreed scope.

Stage 05

Review & handover

Check finishes, resolve outstanding details and hand over a space that works the way it was planned.

The balance

A renovation respects what's there and improves what matters.

A good renovation isn't a rebuild. It's a considered balance between preserving what works and improving what doesn't.

What we preserve
Structural integrity

Existing load-bearing work respected and worked with — not compromised.

What already works well

Layout decisions, natural light, or existing features that still serve the space.

Character of the property

Details that give the space its identity — retained where they still matter.

Existing services

Where they can be reused or upgraded rather than entirely replaced.

What we improve
Function and flow

How people move, work and use the space — made easier and more natural.

Light and comfort

Openings, glazing and surfaces that change how the space feels.

Materials and finishes

New surfaces chosen to suit the property and the intended result.

Practical performance

Storage, services, insulation — the things that make the space work day to day.
