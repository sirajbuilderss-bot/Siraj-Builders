Role definition

Every project role, clearly defined.

Leadership works when everyone knows what they own. This page defines what each role is responsible for, what it isn't, and how the roles connect across a project.

Clear ownership Defined boundaries Connected workflow
01 · Why define roles

Overlap creates confusion. Clarity creates momentum.

Most problems on a construction project aren't technical — they're coordination problems. Two people assumed someone else was handling a decision. A question sat unanswered because it wasn't clear whose answer it was.

Defining roles up front removes that ambiguity. Each role below lists what it owns, what it doesn't, and where it connects to the others.

What this role owns

  • Company-wide standards for quality, conduct and client experience.
  • Final sign-off on the company's strategic and ethical commitments.
  • Overall accountability for how a project is run — even when day-to-day work is delegated.
  • Relationships with key clients and long-standing partners.
  • Escalation point when decisions span multiple projects.

What this role doesn't own

  • Day-to-day site supervision — that belongs to the Site Lead.
  • Individual project schedules and material decisions.
  • Line-by-line drawings and technical specifications.
  • Direct coordination of trades and site labour.
  • Routine client updates — those are handled by the Project Lead.

How roles are assigned

Three principles behind every role.

Single owner per decision

Every important decision has one owner. Others can advise — but ownership doesn't get split.

Adjusted for project size

A larger project may split roles across more people. A smaller one may combine two. The responsibilities don't change.

Confirmed in writing

Roles, contacts and escalation paths are agreed in writing before work begins.
